## Chapter 1: Introduction

### Section 1: General & Application
1. **Objective**: Global instruction aimed at maintaining high customer operational standards, achieving safe cargo handling, and targeting **ZERO damage** across all operations.
2. **Application**:
   - Applies globally to all company-operated "K" Line vessels.
   - Short-term chartered vessels (duration less than 1 year) are exempt from certain specific sections.
3. **Legal Disclaimer**: Standard operating protocols establish baseline safety requirements but do not supersede master/vessel authority in emergency or safety-critical situations.

## Chapter 2: Cargo Handling

### Section 1: General Requirements
1. **"K" Line Representatives**:
   - Port Captain / OBA (Onboard Attendance) represents "K" Line.
   - Representatives wear designated uniform vests / safety clothing.
   - Assists vessel in safe cargo operations prior to port call and during discharge/loading.
2. **Prohibited Actions for Stevedores & Vessel Crew**:
   - 🚫 Drugs and alcohol strictly forbidden.
   - 🚫 Eating, drinking, and smoking prohibited inside cargo holds.
   - 🚫 Personal electronic device usage banned during operations.
   - 🚫 Touching, leaning on, or sitting on cargo is prohibited.
   - 🚫 Unauthorized marking or writing on cargo.
3. **Proper Attire**:
   - Working clothes must be clean, beltless, and buttonless (no exposed metal).
   - No wristwatches, rings, or unshielded accessories allowed (must wear protective wrist covers if worn).
4. **Pre-Cargo Work Meeting**: Mandated meeting between vessel officers and stevedore foremen prior to operation start.

### Section 2: Passenger Car Handling
1. **Breakout Procedures**:
   - Drivers must check display panel gear engagement (automatic vehicles).
   - Driver hands must NOT remain on shift lever during breakout.
   - Stevedores must NOT remove wedges for head-up cars before breakout instruction.
   - Drivers must be prepared to apply parking brakes immediately.
2. **Driving Rules**:
   - Strict **20 km/h (12 mph)** speed limit inside cargo holds.
   - Drive with caution on slopes, ramps, and turning areas.

### Section 3: High & Heavy (H/H) Cargo
1. **Stowage & Protection**:
   - Place rubber protection mats under boom arms, buckets, blades, and heavy contact points.
   - Lower air suspensions on equipped vehicles prior to lashing.
   - Turn main electric switches to **OFF** when fitted.
2. **Trailer Trestles**:
   - Position trailer trestle under semi-trailers after loading; lash trestle using BL 6-ton lashing belts (2 pcs).
   - Replace with wooden dunnage only if trestle is unavailable.

### Section 4: Roll Trailer Cargo
1. **Slope Operations**:
   - Tug master must be positioned on the **down-side** during slope, inner, and outer ramp transit to balance load.
   - Exercise extreme caution and slow speed if down-slope operation requires upside position due to port constraints.
2. **Biosecurity & Quarantine**:
   - Remove all insect eggs, plant matter, BMSB (Brown Marmorated Stink Bug), or AGM (Asian Gypsy Moth) from roll trailers immediately.
   - *Note*: Australian quarantine inspectors may reject empty roll trailers if contamination is found.

### Section 5: Defective Cargo Protocols
Vessel and stevedores must attach official **"DEFECTIVE VEHICLE"** caution labels ticking the applicable condition:
- 🔋 **Dead Battery**: Apply label; arrange jump cart (12V/24V) appropriately.
- 🚜 **Need Towing**: Secure tow bar; notify vessel/stevedore foreman.
- 🛢️ **Oil Leakage**: Place oil tray/absorbent mats under vehicle.
- 🛞 **Flat Tire**: Notify vessel with exact stowage location.
- ⚙️ **Gear Failure**: *Normally prohibited to load*. If loaded, document exact stowage location.
- 🔒 **Door Lock Failure / Locked Cargo / Low Fuel**: Mark label with specific problem description.

## Chapter 3: Lashing Standards

### Section 1: General Requirements
1. **Material Testing & Certification**:
   - All lashing materials must be officially tested and possess valid certificates stored in the Cargo Securing Manual (CSM).
   - New or alternative lashing materials require test records and valid certification submitted to CTQ.

### Section 2: Standard Number of Lashings
- **Passenger Cars**:
  - BL 2.0 ton belts: Vessel's RT capacity × 4.15
  - BL 2.4 ton belts: Vessel's RT capacity × 3.70
- **Belt Straps**: RT capacity × 0.8 × 3.5
- **Heavy Cargo (BL 6.0 ton belts)**: Main deck RT capacity × 3.5 + HH deck RT capacity × 3.5 / 2
- **Chains (SWL 3.75t / 5.0t)**: Applied for heavy machinery based on weight matrix.

### Section 3: Replacement & Scrapping Criteria
1. **Anti-Falling Plate Color Coding (BL 2.4t Belts)**:
   - **2020–2021**: BLUE
   - **2022–2023**: WHITE
   - **2024–2025**: RED
   - **2026–2027**: GREEN
   - **2028–2029**: YELLOW
   - **2030–2031**: BLUE
2. **Scrapping Thresholds**: Immediately isolate and scrap belts with:
   - Cut damage > 2mm width or > 10mm length.
   - Damaged seams or broken stitching threads.
   - Widespread friction burns or severe oil contamination.
   - Unreadable SWL labels/stamps.

## Chapter 4: Cargo Management & Safety

### Section 1: Hold Safety & Walking Protocols
1. **Safe Walking Rules**:
   - Walk on designated paths along bulkheads/walls in single file.
   - Finger-point before crossing active vehicle transit routes.
   - 🚫 Do NOT walk on active car running routes.
   - 🚫 Do NOT cross inner ramps or step into turning blind spots.

### Section 2: Battery Electric Vehicle (BEV) Safety
1. **Monitoring**:
   - Patrol cargo holds and monitor BEV temperatures using thermal cameras / non-contact thermometers.
   - Report any BEV showing temperature > **50°C** or noticeably higher than surrounding vehicles immediately.
2. **Required Equipment**:
   - Vessels carrying BEVs must carry at least one certified portable gas detector capable of detecting Hydrocarbons (HC), Carbon Monoxide (CO), and Oxygen (O2).

### Section 3: Damage Prevention & Caution Labels
- **"ROOF HIT"**: Posted on Toyota Coaster/Hi-Ace and high-roof units near low clearance decks.
- **"LOW SPOILER"**: Posted on low-clearance sports cars (first car in block or every individual car).
- **"GO STRAIGHT"**: Attached near pillars and tight stowage locations.
- **"BREAK OUT"**: Attached to cars requiring guided signalman breakout.

## Chapter 6: Damage Case Studies & Prevention

### Case 1: Scratched Bumper Damage
- **Situation**: Toyota damage analysis shows scratches on front/rear bumpers are the most frequent cargo damage.
- **Cause**: Stevedore claspers dangling loosely or striking soft bumper materials during lashing/unlashing.
- **Prevention**: Hold claspers firmly; enforce buttonless/beltless dress code; exercise care near vehicle perimeters.

### Case 2: Pillar Blind Spot Damage
- **Situation**: Vehicle parked in temporary position near a pillar hit the pillar during discharge.
- **Cause**: A-pillar and dangling shipping paperwork created a blind spot for the driver.
- **Prevention**: Drivers must inspect surroundings outside the vehicle before entry and maintain vigilance when turning near pillars.

### Case 3: Slippery Floor Control Loss
- **Situation**: Rain and mud brought in by tires created slippery patches on deck floors; taxi driver lost control and hit a pillar.
- **Cause**: Excessive speed on wet, dirty hold surfaces.
- **Prevention**: Adhere strictly to < 20 km/h speed limits; vessel crew must mop and clean water/dirt patches promptly.
